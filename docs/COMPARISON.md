# The field — the other Real World Assets entries, and the exact boundary

Read from the public BUIDL gallery of the Build with CMC: API Hackathon on 2026-09-19 (23
BUIDLs visible; the five in the Real World Assets track, plus one RWA-subject agent entered in
the AI Agents track). One line each on what it does and where Shelfware stops and it begins.
None of the six overlap on the row that matters — the wrapper with `price: null`.

| BUIDL | What it is | The boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Backstop** (48946) | Issuer concentration by *value*: attributes market cap per token from `tokens[]`, measures how concentrated it is (Herfindahl, top-N shares, per chain and asset class), reconciles its per-token totals against `assets/list` on every run, and publishes the holes in the data beside the figures. Snapshots archived by a GitHub Actions cron. | Backstop's own page states it: *"almost half the tokens report no market cap at all, so every share is a share of the half that does."* The half it sets aside is exactly the half Shelfware counts. Shelfware never weights by value and never sums a market cap into a share — it joins each wrapper to its listing state on `/v1/cryptocurrency/map` and grades issuers by the share of their wrappers with no CMC-tracked market. Same universe (Backstop read 790 tokened underlyings on its day, Shelfware 791 on 2026-09-18), opposite halves of the roster. Both surface the `num_tokens` over-declaration (Backed 1,176 declared vs 772 attached) and both publish both numbers. |
| **Bedrock** (48952) | A research explorer over the RWA family: the top 1,000 assets screened by tokenised market cap, asset detail with SEC EDGAR links, a live wrapper-spread ranking across the top 80, issuer and chain share, "New & Upcoming" onboarding additions, a terminal, watchlists and CSV export. | Bedrock ranks and explores by price and market cap — a wrapper priced `null` cannot enter a spread ranking or a cap-weighted share, so it is filtered out before the screen is drawn. Its already-tokenised / not-yet split is the map's `has_tokens` boolean; Shelfware starts from that boolean and contradicts it wrapper by wrapper: of the 791 flagged tokenised, 476 have no wrapper with a market. |
| **Parity** (48797) | Fair-price and wrapper-spread layer for tokenised stocks: a blended wrapper price against the underlying's own print, every wrapper ranked cheapest to dearest, 180 sessions of premium history reconstructed from OHLCV, verdicts (OVERPRICED / FAIR / ILLIQUID…), and the same engine as five MCP tools. | Parity's eligibility pipeline runs *presence → unit → liquidity → outlier* before any verdict, so a wrapper with no price is excluded at step one — every row Shelfware counts is a row Parity excludes by design. Parity answers *"is the price right?"* for the wrappers that have one; Shelfware answers *"is there a market at all, and whose?"* for all 1,435. |
| **Signal Desk** (48805) | A market-posture desk over 20 endpoints — risk appetite from market cap direction against BTC dominance, breadth of participation, sector rotation, and the disagreement between sentiment and participation — with a tokenised-stock explorer (company → tokens → issuers, inverted into a ranked issuer view) and a live capability probe that classifies each endpoint as ok / plan-gated. | Its issuer view ranks issuers by what they issued, read from the same `tokens[]`. Shelfware's issuer table is those rows joined to the one ledger the RWA family does not carry — the cryptocurrency map's listing state — so each issuer is graded by how many of its wrappers have no market, beside the live market cap of the ones that do. Signal Desk is wide (regime, breadth, sectors, RWA); Shelfware is one verb on one screen. |
| **RWA Compass** (48802) | An LLM agent (tool-calling, with a rule-based router when no model key is set) with three tools: asset lookup and plain-English explainer, a liquidity/risk snapshot that labels venue concentration Thin / Concentrated / Reasonable from per-exchange volume, and a two-asset comparison — every answer with its raw figures beneath it. | Compass labels the liquidity of wrappers that trade, from the venues they trade on; a wrapper with no CMC-tracked market has no venue rows to concentrate, so it sits outside Compass's question. Shelfware has no model in it: every number is a count over committed rows that a judge re-derives with two `jq` filters. |
| **Will RWA Let Me In** (48575, AI Agents track) | An agent that joins five RWA endpoints to a curated registry of vault access terms (jurisdiction, investor status, minimums, fees, redemption) and prepares unsigned deposit and redemption requests for an IXS vault on Avalanche that the user signs in their own wallet. | It answers *"can I get in?"* — eligibility and vault mechanics, half of it from a hand-curated registry. Shelfware answers the question before that one — *is there anything with a market to get into?* — from CoinMarketCap's rows alone, nothing curated, no wallet, no chain. Different question, different data, different track. |

**What none of them count:** 476 of 791 tokenised underlyings (60%) have no wrapper with a
CMC-tracked market; Backed Assets' 772-wrapper catalogue is 82% shelf; Dinari's is 100%. The
`has_tokens: true` flag and the `status: untracked` state come from the same API, and no other
entry puts them side by side.

**What they do that Shelfware does not, deliberately:** price charts, wrapper-vs-underlying
spreads and premiums, cap-weighted issuer shares, venue concentration, vault access terms,
portfolios, watchlists, a chat surface. Those are their lanes; the scope here is one verb —
count the shelf — and one screen.
